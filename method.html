Metrics have regimes; price the path

Sep 2023

The problem with most performance metrics are the stickiness we attribute to them, as if they're more sustainable than the portfolios they measure.

Step in regime or structural changes.
Such reliance can lead to understatement of emerging risks.
Metrics have regimes too.
In quantifying the markets, the problem is not in the data or the math, its in the interpretation.

Two equally thoughtful speculators can look at the same data; one sees a sell (overdone, reversal pending) and the other sees a buy (strong trend established).

Interpret wisely.

The problem with claims that OTM options can be cheap is that the forecast error resulting from sparse tail data exceeds the possible valuation range. The pricing error can literally be larger than the price.

Buying OTM options isn't a function of value, its a function of need.
An option's premium is priced by the probability that its strike will be profitable at expiration.
But a single probability at one moment in time squeezes a vast opportunity set into a statistical corner.
In reality, each outcome takes a wild path through time.
Price the path.
Which trade is "best"?

Ironically, every trade is a trade off. Whether a choice of ratio, of direction, of factor or sector tilt, etc.. there's always a benefit and a cost.

Point is, there's no free lunch; there's only deciding on the goal, and with that, which risk to accept.
The value of boring:

In markets, doing nothing can seem boring, whereas actively choosing to do nothing is very much doing something. Trading too frequently, as a reach for more excitement, is a common driver of losses.

Boring is very often the better approach. Embrace boring.

I used to trade any pattern I could see. I would trade 30 to 35 patterns a month. Now, I am much more selective. I used to trade patterns like symmetrical triangles and trendlines, which I no longer do. I only trade patterns where the breakout is through a horizontal boundary.

Why is that?
With horizontal boundaries, you can find out much more quickly whether you are right or wrong.
Was there a catalyst for that change?

No, it was just a gradual process of realizing that I was getting my best results trading rectangles, ascending triangles, and descending triangles. Give me a 10-week rectangle with a well-defined boundary that ends with a wide daily bar breakout out of that pattern, and now we’re cooking.

Every process has an edge only sometimes

Nov 2023

Every process has an edge in certain situations.. not all.. and we need to be aware and watch that proactively

Your instruments should measure what matters for your strategy. Warren Buffet doesn’t care about 1-month realized volatility any more than a flow trader should care about AAPL margins. Your dashboards should be windows into your actions. The select group of tools in your pencil box must survive ruthless interrogation. Someone spouts some data point and your response should be “Thanks for the cocktail trivia but does that even matter?”
